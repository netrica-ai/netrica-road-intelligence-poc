1 · Use case and approach

Road owners and their consultants increasingly hold mobile-LiDAR surveys and "digital twins" of their networks, but the intelligence on top of that data is still produced by hand: asset inventories, usable carriageway width, vertical clearance, encroachment and norm compliance are digitised point by point. Netrica automates that layer. From an existing survey, with no additional field work, the pipeline returns a per-station corridor-geometry profile, the asset classes along it, standards flags against Indian norms, and a per-vehicle passability verdict that can gate routing.

Approach in one sentence. Segment the point cloud with a Point Transformer V3 foundation encoder adapted to Indian streets; measure the carriageway from the road points themselves (not from lane markings); measure overhead clearance per obstruction stream; fuse width and clearance into standards-based classes; transfer the result to the road graph; and deliver it as GIS layers and a static web application the owner can host.

Why it is different. (i) Width and clearance are assessed together against the vehicles that must pass; (ii) cross-city deployment uses model merging, so the model travels and the data stays; (iii) a training-free junction detector handles irregular Indian geometry; (iv) every output carries its evidence and a human sign-off step.

2 · System architecture

1 · IngestLAS/LAZ + vehicle trajectory. Tiling (5 m), ground filtering, trajectory-based corridor extraction. Any MLS platform.
2 · SegmentPTv3 foundation encoder (Sonata / Concerto / Utonia) fine-tuned per city; merged in weight space for a new city; 12-class taxonomy (62 categories in Bhopal).
3 · MeasureRoad occupancy → boundary → medial centreline → junctions (orientation-field defects) → width per station; clearance per stream; multi-drive fusion.
4 · CertifyIRC / CMVR tiers W0–W4, H0–H3, A0–A3; per-vehicle code; alignment to the OSM graph; accessibility-gated routing.
5 · DeliverGeoJSON / GeoPackage, 3D Tiles, classified PLY scenes, routing graph JSON, static web app, PDF audit; REST endpoint on request.

Stages 1–4 run as a batch pipeline (Python / PyTorch) on the owner's GPU host or ours; stage 5 is a static site (Vite + TypeScript) that reads the exported files, so there is no server-side component to secure or scale. The three-city PoC on this site is exactly that export.

Hand-off format for a partner corridor

LAS/LAZ 1.2–1.4 with GPS time, intensity and (if available) RGB; the vehicle trajectory (SBET/CSV, or GPS-time-tagged positions); the CRS (EPSG code); nominal density ≥ 100 pts/m² for the width chain, higher preferred for wires. Imagery is optional and, when supplied, is anonymised before use.

4 · Technical implementation, stage by stage

4.1 Ingest and representation

  • Tiling into 5 m scenes along the trajectory; 25 cm voxel protocol for training statistics; width is measured at full resolution because subsampling shifts kerb-to-kerb extremes by up to a voxel at exactly the 3.0–3.5 m thresholds.
  • Nine input channels: xyz, RGB (zero-filled where the survey has none), intensity, normals. Colour channels are therefore optional.
  • No denoising: three outlier filters were scored against hand-traced polygons and rejected (they removed boundary points and worsened width error).

4.2 Semantic segmentation and cross-city deployment

  • Encoders: Point Transformer V3 family foundation models (Sonata, Concerto, Utonia) via Pointcept; protocols evaluated: linear probe, decoder probe, full fine-tuning. Deployed: fine-tuned Utonia / Concerto per city.
  • Cross-city deployment by multi-city model merging: task vectors (fine-tuned minus shared pre-trained initialisation) combined in weight space; the encoder is merged and each target city's segmentation head re-attached. The merged model has the same inference cost as a single model. A shared pre-trained initialisation is required (violating it collapses the merge).
  • The deployed configuration is selected on accessibility fidelity (agreement of the resulting W/H/A map with the hand-label run), not on mIoU alone.

4.3 Carriageway boundary, centreline, junctions and width

  • Road-class points → occupancy mask → hole filling (parked-vehicle scan shadows) → boundary extraction and refinement; divided carriageways are split at the median by a class-aware carve.
  • Medial-axis centreline; stations every 5–10 m; width read as the kerb-to-kerb extreme across the station transect. Junction-mouth flares and sparse-shoulder balloons are removed as outliers (a reading wider than 1.4× the 35th percentile of its neighbours within 4 m, or 1.6× the median near a junction); narrow readings are never dropped because a genuine pinch must survive; readings under 1 m are raster artefacts.
  • Junctions are detected as topological defects in a nematic orientation field over the road points (scalar order parameter for directional coherence; Poincaré index for defect charge). A through-road has high order; an intersection is where order breaks and a half-integer defect appears. Types: T/Y, 4-way (+), skew (X), roundabout. Training-free; outperforms a detector on learned 3D features (quality 0.671 vs 0.548).
  • Width-uncertain (WU) policy: side streets the scanner only grazes have a boundary set by the survey footprint, not the road; such stations are reported as WU, excluded from certification and routing.

4.4 Overhead clearance and binding object

  • Three streams above the drivable band: wire, structure (shop shades, boards, bridges, connectors), vegetation. Clearance at a station = lowest object in the band; the binding stream and the binding object class (from the 62-category taxonomy in Bhopal) are recorded.
  • Wires: per-span quadratic sag fit on spans ≥ 5 m, accepted at RMSE ≤ 0.25 m; gaps ≤ 15 m bridged; isolated returns removed by a statistical filter kept as a visible layer in the audit viewer.
  • Critical stations (where clearance crosses a vehicle threshold) keep their point evidence so an engineer can inspect the exact returns.

4.5 Multi-drive reconciliation

Where a street was driven more than once, drives are conflated into one connected single-line graph; the fused width is the minimum safe reading across drives (closer repaired, excluded endpoints ringed). Transient narrowing (parked or moving vehicles) is attributed by a ground-cause label and a transient level T0–T3 on hand-labelled drives.

4.6 Network transfer and routing

  • The LiDAR centreline is aligned to the OSM road graph (raw and aligned OSM both shown in the app); each OSM edge receives the minimum width and clearance along it and a 3-bit block code per vehicle class (width / wire-structure / vegetation).
  • Routing: shortest, fastest and accessibility-gated variants by Dijkstra or A* over the exported graph; unsurveyed edges are passable by policy (uncertainty never manufactures a block) and the share of a route on unsurveyed road is reported.
  • In the application the routing runs client-side in TypeScript (src/routing/graph.ts), 0.25–2.5 ms per query on 250–1,400-node city graphs.

6 · The delivered application

  • Home (index.html): live KPI tiles computed in the browser from the served station layers; the network map with click-to-route; the classified 3D scene explorer; the embedded corridor audit; deployment notes.
  • Network map: CesiumJS, 2D/3D, OSM or satellite basemap; city selection; OSM ↔ LiDAR alignment overlay; Categorize by W / H / A class; fused-width overlay (Bhopal); 🚑 Route with class, gate and algorithm selectors; info cards with measured values; full screen.
  • 3D scenes: three.js PLY viewer with class ↔ RGB toggle, click-to-identify, two-point distance measurement, eye-dome depth shading; three themed scenes per city.
  • Corridor audit (validate.html): per-path, step-by-step replay of the width chain, the clearance chain and the fused verdict, with ground-truth overlays and a Deployment panel on predicted labels; keyboard navigation between paths.
  • Formats served: GeoJSON (EPSG:32643) station, junction, centreline, OSM-aligned and categorisation layers; routing graph JSON; classified PLY; 3D Tiles; JSON manifests.
{
  "type": "Feature",
  "geometry": { "type": "LineString", "coordinates": [...] },   // one 5–10 m station, EPSG:32643
  "properties": { "path": "Bhopal_1", "wm": 6.99, "w": 4, "hm": 3.94, "h": 2, "a": 2,
                  "bind": "Wire", "bstream": "wire" }             // width m/class, clearance m/class, A-class, binding object
}

Routing-graph edges carry len_m, speed, and per-class block bits (1 = width, 2 = wire/structure, 4 = vegetation). A GeoPackage export with the same schema is produced by the batch pipeline for GIS users.

8 · Data privacy, protection and compliance

  • No personal data in the product. The pipeline's inputs are LiDAR geometry (and optionally intensity/colour); its outputs are geometry, classes and derived measures. No names, identifiers, trajectories of persons, number plates or faces are stored or served.
  • Imagery is anonymised before use. Where street-level imagery is used in development, faces, heads and number plates are detected (SAM 3, score ≥ 0.30 plus a head band on every person) and mosaicked/blurred; coloured points whose colour came from a blurred pixel are recoloured, and points predicted as persons have their colour zeroed. The build refuses to publish any raster or cloud without a current blurred copy (a hash-checked gate), and a separate gate verifies that no person points in the served scenes carry colour.
  • Data sovereignty. Cross-city capability is transferred by model merging; raw survey data never leaves its owner. The delivery layer can be hosted entirely on the owner's infrastructure. This is consistent with the Guidelines for acquiring and producing Geospatial Data and Geospatial Data Services (DST, 2021) for Indian entities and with the data-minimisation principle of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
  • Ethics. The Bhopal data collection is covered by an IISER Bhopal Department Level Ethics Committee approval (IISERB/DLEC/2026-27/06, 15 September 2026).
  • Responsible AI. Every automated flag carries its measured evidence, the binding object and a standards class; a compliance verdict is signed off by an engineer in the audit viewer before it is issued. Uncertainty is reported, never converted into a block (unsurveyed road is passable by policy and labelled as such). Models are trained on three urban forms to limit single-city bias; the data-source and assumption log travels with each report.
  • Application security. Static files only; no accounts, cookies, trackers or analytics; search engines are asked not to index the PoC. Access control, if required, is applied at the host (IP allow-list or basic auth).

10 · Plan for a Rodic corridor

  1. Day 0–1 · Data in. Rodic shares a LAS/LAZ sample with trajectory and CRS (5–20 km; Mumbai twin or an OPRMC stretch) under NDA; we confirm density and coverage and return a tiling report.
  2. Day 1–3 · Segmentation. Merged encoder for the new city; spot check of the 12 classes with Rodic engineers; optional short fine-tune if Rodic supplies labelled stretches.
  3. Day 3–6 · Measurement and certification. Width, clearance, binding object, junctions, standards flags, per-vehicle passability; multi-drive fusion where repeated; transfer to the road graph.
  4. Day 6–8 · Deliverables. The web application on the corridor (same UI as this PoC), GeoJSON / GeoPackage layers, the per-object asset register (class, position, height, offset from centreline) as GeoPackage, a PDF audit, and a validation note against a Rodic-nominated sample stretch.

Acceptance targets (from our application): asset detection precision/recall above 0.85 on the major classes; width and clearance within ±0.15 m of manual measurement on a sample stretch; end-to-end processing under one hour per 10 km on a single GPU. Status labels used throughout the PoC: live running on this site · next scheduled for the corridor run · in development not certified.
